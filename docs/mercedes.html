One share price,
two completely
different stories

1,105 trading sessions between January 2020 and April 2024, and a headline
return that conceals everything interesting about the period.

Executive summary

Between 2 January 2020 and 30 April 2024 the share went from $41.57 to $70.94: a return of 70.7%. Reported on its own, that number is close to useless.

Inside the same window the stock fell 56.3% in ten weeks, bottoming at $18.17 on 19 March 2020, then rose 322% from that low to a high of $76.61 on 8 April 2024. An investor who bought at the March low roughly quadrupled their money. An investor who bought on the first trading day of 2020 waited two years to return to break-even.

Same instrument, same four years, opposite experiences — determined entirely by a start date.

Why this matters for reporting

A single-figure return computed between two arbitrary endpoints is the most common way a chart misleads without containing a false number. Everything in the row above is true; only the combination is honest.

For a shareholder communication the useful framing is not how much did it grow but what would it have taken to hold it. This position required surviving a 56% decline in under three months. Any presentation of the 70.7% that omits the drawdown is describing an outcome nobody actually experienced.

What we would do

  1. Never report a period return without its maximum drawdown. They belong on the same line.
  2. Anchor commentary on the recovery, not the endpoints — the 322% from the March 2020 low is the event, and it is a story about the shock, not about steady growth.
  3. Show the two-year break-even for anyone who held from the start of the period. It is the number a real holder felt.

Method

Daily OHLC and adjusted close, 1,105 sessions. Drawdown computed as the largest decline from any running peak to a subsequent close across the full series, rather than from the calendar-year high.

What this does not tell you

This is price behaviour, not company performance — nothing here speaks to revenue, margin or competitive position. Returns are computed on close prices and exclude dividends, so total return to a shareholder is understated. The window begins ten weeks before a market-wide shock, which shapes every figure in it; a different start date produces a materially different report, which is precisely the point being made. None of this is investment advice.
